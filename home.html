About

I'm a doctoral student in the Ecology and Evolutionary Biology Department at Binghamton University, where I work in the Grames Lab for Insect Ecology and Conservation. I’m an ecologist working to improve and leverage the tools necessary to monitor wildlife and environmental change. Currently my work focuses on using historical bioacoustics data to inform insect conservation. I’m also developing computational methods using GIS data for optimized site selection to build monitoring networks from fine to broad scales. My taxa of expertise is birds and bugs, but I’m always interested in expanding my skillset to whatever research question or conservation management problem I come across. I also love kayaking, fly fishing, and hanging out with my dog.

Please feel free to reach out if I can be of any help or service to you, or connect with me through the channels listed on this website.

Background

I hold a M.S. in Biology from Commonwealth University of Pennsylvania (2023) and a B.S. in Wildlife and Environmental Biology from Keystone College (2021). As my research has shifted my skillset has shifted with it—typically rooted in conservation management. During my B.S. I worked in trail maintenance and as a maple syrup producer. As I was finishing my B.S. I took a seasonal technician role at the USDA Forest Service where I learned bird identification, banding, and surveying techniques, and surveyed streams for Wood Turtles. Later I surveyed birds as part of a PhD project measuring community response to dynamic forest management. From these early experiences I started to love the research vibe! Most of the biologists I worked for had graduate degrees, and I wanted to contribute at the leadership level to decision making about species conservation and protecting our natural resources, so I decided to continue my education.

I was interested in ecotoxicology-specifically microplastic pollution and decided to pursue a M.S. that sought to measure microplastic loading in terrestrial birds using diet analysis methods from bird banding samples, and compare it to commonly used necropsies. My M.S. research introduced a lot of questions about how to improve and generalize monitoring iniatives across conservation issues, and I also started to consider trophically lower taxa like insects, which are important trophically for most birds and experience many of the same challenges as birds, but often with less conservation attention! During this time I was also branching out into bioacoustics-monitoring Saw-whet Owl vocalizations and perforing audio analysis on song bird recordings. The culmination of these questions, considerations, and interests brought me to the Grames Lab, where we've been really excited about pursuing some of these questions related to improving and adding to environmental monitoring initiatives, and using bioacoustics to improve our understanding of sound-producing insect communities.

I am pursuing a career in teaching and/or research, either at an academic or other institution. Once strictly a field biologist, I've become comfortable both in the field collectiong data and behind a computer doing analysis or modeling. I mostly work in R, although I am slowly learning Python and Jupyter Notebook as needed, and I have dabbled with some computer vision applications for morphological ID of moths. All of my publications, posters, and presentations are posted on my ResearchGate, and my code is posted on my Github for open access viewing.
