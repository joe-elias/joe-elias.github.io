About

I'm a PhD student in the Ecology and Evolutionary Biology Department at Binghamton University, where I work in The Grames Lab for Insect Conservation and Ecology. My focus is applied conservation science, and I’m broadly interested in global change and environmental contaminants and how these drivers impact ecological communities. I currently work on data science and machine learning methods for automating recognition of acoustic patterns, image analysis, and geospatial modeling to assist conservation management. I love being in the field as well, collecting data on birds and arthropods by sound and capture (with permits). I'm currently diving into molecular methods for capturing and leveraging environmental DNA to help with biomonitoring initiatives, so stay tuned on this endeavor!

Check out this website portfolio or the Orthoptera Docs if you're interested in following our bioacoustics projects. Please reach out if you would like to talk about anything I have going on, or if my skills can be of service to you. When I'm not out in the field, listening to soundscape recordings, or writing code I'm usually hanging out with my dog, Indie :)

Background

I hold a M.S. in Biology from Commonwealth University of Pennsylvania (2023) and a B.S. in Wildlife and Environmental Biology from Keystone College (2021). During my B.S. I worked in trail maintenance and as a maple sugaring producer. After my B.S., I worked as a field technician surveying birds and forest vegetation to measure community response to dynamic forest management. I was also a seasonal technician for the USDA helping with songbird banding and surveys for Northern Goshawks, and stream surveys for Wood Turtles. With the USDA I had my first lead researcher role examining the nest box use and breeding occupancy of Northern Saw-whet Owls, a small and cryptic owl in the Strigidae family that holds a special place in my heart. I continued working with Saw-whets through my M.S., documenting their breeding behavior in reclaimed habitat and still help with owl banding at the Lackawanna Avian Research Center when I can.

My interests in pollution and ecotoxicology led me to my M.S. project researching microplastic ingestion and retenton in landbirds. Figuring out ways to limit plastic-use and lethal methods when possible in science is important, so I developed a novel workflow for collection and analysis of microplastics in fecal samples from bird banding, and performed necropsies exclusively on birds already deceased from window and vehicle collisions.

During my my M.S., I became really interested in helping standardize and improve monitoring initiatives for species conservation and global change. Shortly after I was converted from ornithology by the entomologists...don't get me wrong, I still love my birds but it is undeniable how utterly important insects are for every ecosystem, and they often experience many of the same and more conservation problems as birds but with so much less attention. And they are pretty awesome too!

My search for large-scale conservation action and collaborative monitoring initiatives brought me to the The Grames Lab at Binghamton University in 2024. Since then we've been working on some really cool collaborations to improve large-scale moth biodiversity monitoring, computational modeling approaches to decrease bias in designing monitoring networks, and using bioacoustics to improve our understanding of sound-producing Orthopteran communities and their response to environmental change and restoration management.
