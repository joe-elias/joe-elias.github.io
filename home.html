About

I'm a doctoral student in the Ecology and Evolutionary Biology Department at Binghamton University, where I work in the Grames Lab for Insect Ecology and Conservation. I am an ecologist working on developing and leveraging non-lethal tools to monitor wildlife and environmental change. Currently my work focuses on using historical bioacoustics data to inform insect conservation. I’m also developing geospatial computational methods for optimized site selection to help build monitoring networks from fine to broad scales. My taxa of expertise is birds and insects, but I’m always interested in expanding my skillset to whatever research question or conservation management problem I come across. I also love kayaking, fly fishing, and hanging out with my dog.

Please feel free to reach out if I can be of any help or service to you, or connect with me through the channels listed on this website.

Background

I hold a M.S. in Biology from Commonwealth University of Pennsylvania (2023) and a B.S. in Wildlife and Environmental Biology from Keystone College (2021). During my B.S. I worked in trail maintenance and as a maple syrup producer. As I was finishing my B.S. I took a seasonal technician role at the U.S. Department of Agriculture where I learned bird identification, banding, and surveying techniques, and surveyed streams for Wood Turtles. Later I surveyed birds as a technician on a PhD project measuring community response to dynamic forest management. I started to love every stage of research, and I wanted to contribute at the leadership level to decision making about species and natural resourses conservation so I decided to continue my education.

I was interested in plastic pollution and loved birds, so I pursued a M.S. that sought to measure microplastic loading in terrestrial birds using diet analysis methods from bird banding samples. I compared the non-lethal samples to commonly used necropsies on deceased specimens (collected from window collisions). My M.S. research introduced a lot of questions about improving collaborative monitoring iniatives across conservation issues, and the importance of insects, which are important trophically for most birds and experience many of the same challenges as birds, but often with less conservation attention! During this time I was also branching out into bioacoustics. I was monitoring Saw-whet owl vocalizations and performing audio analysis on song bird recordings. The culmination of these questions, considerations, and interests brought me to the Grames Lab, where we've been really excited about pursuing some of these questions related to improving and adding to environmental monitoring initiatives, and using bioacoustics to improve our understanding of sound-producing insect communities.

I am pursuing a career in teaching and/or research, either at an academic or other institution. Once strictly a field biologist, I've become comfortable both in the field collectiong data and behind a computer doing analysis or modeling. I mostly work in R, although I am slowly learning Python and Jupyter Notebook as needed, and I have dabbled with some computer vision applications for morphometric ID of moths. All of my publications, posters, and presentations are posted on my ResearchGate, and most of my code is posted on my Github for open access viewing.
