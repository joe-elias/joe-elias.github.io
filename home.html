About

I'm a PhD student in the Ecology and Evolutionary Biology Department at Binghamton University, where I work in The Grames Lab for Insect Conservation and Ecology. My focus is applied conservation science, and I’m broadly interested in global change and environmental contaminants and how these drivers impact ecological communities. I currently work on data science and machine learning methods for automating recognition of acoustic patterns, image analysis, and geospatial modeling to assist conservation management. I love being in the field as well, collecting data on birds and arthropods by sound and capture (with permits). I'm currently diving into molecular methods for capturing and leveraging environmental DNA to help with biomonitoring initiatives, so stay tuned on this endeavor!

Check out this website portfolio or the Orthoptera Docs if you're interested in following our bioacoustics projects. Please reach out if you would like to talk about anything I have going on, or if my skills can be of service to you. When I'm not out in the field, listening to soundscape recordings, or writing code I'm usually hanging out with my dog, Indie :)

Background

I hold a M.S. in Biology from Commonwealth University of Pennsylvania (2023) and a B.S. in Wildlife and Environmental Biology from Keystone College (2021). During my B.S. I worked in trail maintenance and as a maple sugaring producer on a 170-acre nature preserve. I was a seasonal technician for the USDA in the Allegheny National Forest, where I helped with songbird banding and surveys for Northern Goshawks, and stream surveys for Wood Turtles. With the USDA I had my first lead role on a research project examining the nest box use and breeding occupancy of Northern Saw-whet Owls, a small and cryptic owl in the Strigidae family that holds a special place in my heart. I continued working with Saw-whets through my M.S., documenting their breeding behavior in reclaimed habitat and still help with owl banding at the Lackawanna Avian Research Center whenever I can.

My M.S. research focused on microplastics ingestion and retenton in terrestrial birds using necropsies and diet analysis from non-lethal collection methods. This research introduced me to a lot of questions about improving and standardizing collaborative monitoring iniatives across conservation issues. One issue that I became passionate about was insect decline and conservation. Insects are important trophically for most birds and experience many of the same challenges as birds, but often with less conservation attention!

During this time I was also branching out into using bioacoustics to monitor Saw-whet owl breeding behavior and performing audio analysis on song bird recordings for the Kitzes Lab at the University of Pittsburgh. The culmination of these questions, considerations, and interests brought me to the Grames Lab, where we've been pursuing some of these questions related to developing and bolstering infrastructure and inference for biodiveristy monitoring, and using bioacoustics to improve our understanding of sound-producing Orthopteran communities and their response to environmental change and restoration management.
