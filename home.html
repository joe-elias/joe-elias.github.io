About

I'm a doctoral student in the Ecology and Evolutionary Biology Department at Binghamton University, where I work in the Grames Lab for Insect Ecology and Conservation. My focus is applied conservation science and general ecology. I mainly work on using data science and machine learning for bioacoustics, image analysis, and geospatial modeling. I love community ecology as well - mainly collecting field data on songbirds by sound and capture (with permits), and arthropods. I'm currently diving into molecular methods for capturing and leveraging environmental DNA to monitor arthropod diveristy so stay tuned on this endeavor.

Check out this website portfolio and please reach out if you would like to talk about anything I have going on, or if my skills can be of service to you. P.S. Here is a picture of my dog, Indie :)

Background

I hold a M.S. in Biology from Commonwealth University of Pennsylvania (2023) and a B.S. in Wildlife and Environmental Biology from Keystone College (2021). During my B.S. I worked in trail maintenance and as a maple syrup producer. During college I worked as a seasonal technician for the U.S. Department of Agriculture where I learned bird identification, banding, and nest surveys, and stream surveys for Wood Turtles. Later I took a technician role surveying birds and trees on a PhD project measuring community response to dynamic forest management. I loved the research exposure I had up until this point, and I wanted to contribute at the leadership level to decision making about species and natural resourses conservation so I decided to pursue a master's degree.

I pursued an M.S. project that sought to measure microplastic loading in terrestrial birds using diet analysis methods from bird banding samples. I compared the non-lethal sampling method to commonly used necropsies on deceased specimens (collected from window collisions). My M.S. research introduced me to a lot of questions about improving and standardizing collaborative monitoring iniatives across conservation issues. One conservation issue that peaked my interest was insect decline and conservation. Insects are important trophically for most birds and experience many of the same challenges as birds, but often with less conservation attention! During this time I was also branching out into bioacoustics. I was monitoring Saw-whet owl vocalizations and performing audio analysis on song bird recordings. The culmination of these questions, considerations, and interests brought me to the Grames Lab, where we've been really excited about pursuing some of these questions related to improving and adding to environmental monitoring initiatives, and using bioacoustics to improve our understanding of sound-producing insect communities.

I am pursuing a career in teaching and/or research, either at an academic or other institution. Once strictly a field biologist, I've become comfortable both in the field collectiong data and behind a computer doing analysis or modeling. I mostly work in R, although I am learning Python to develop machine learning models and other computer vision applications for morphometric ID of moths. Also, I know enough html to make this website...for what it's worth. All of my publications, posters, and presentations are posted on my ResearchGate and most of my code is posted on my Github for open access viewing. Both resources are linked on this webpage.
