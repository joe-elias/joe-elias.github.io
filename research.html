🦗 Restoring a historical multi‑decade time series of nocturnal passive acoustic monitoring

Bioacoustics—the study of sound production within ecosystems—enables researchers to efficiently track ecological change by measuring the natural communication signals produced by wildlife. Although widely used for studying birds, bioacoustics remains underutilized for insects. Orthoptera play essential roles in nutrient cycling, food‑web support, and bioindication. Declines at an estimated rate of 2.1–2.7% per year in Orthoptera abundance threaten these ecosystem services, yet they remain far less studied than most other insect groups. One major barrier to routine passive acoustic monitoring (PAM) of sound‑producing insects is the scarcity of recordings collected under natural field conditions which is crucial for rapid and standardized species identification. I am working to restore and analyze a historical PAM dataset totaling ~7,000 hours (25 years) of consistent soundscape recordings replicated across eight sites. This project involves digitizing and restoring nine years of analog cassette recordings, conducting audio analysis and annotation, and customizing a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) to automate species detection. The resulting dataset will be used to infer long-term trends in Orthoptera biodiversity and potential environmental drivers of change.

🗺️📍 An algorithmic approach for optimal site selection to help design ecological monitoring networks

Monitoring networks are vital for tracking change within and between ecosystems. However, many monitoring networks have bias introduced at the research design phase which can skew inference. I am developing an algorithmic approach to quantify and account for bias during site selection under common geographic and environmental constraints for representative ecological monitoring initiatives. The final product can be used to establish new site networks as well as update and quanitfy the optimization of existing networks to help better understand the resulting data. This method uses GIS data and GIS tools in R (via the `siteselectr` package). Code and workflows are available on GitHub, with a GUI option (coming soon!).

🐦🧫Microplastic Loading in Terrestrial Birds from Non‑Lethal Sampling

The accumulation of microplastics is a global problem that is increasing for a widening list of taxonomic groups and trophic levels. There is little research on microplastic loading in terrestrial birds despite their usefulness as indicators to measure the ecological effects of pollution by virtue of their diverse behavior and morphology, as well as their relative ease to study. We tested whether MP were present in terrestrial birds ranging in size from 7-36 cm via two methods of non-lethal sample collection: (1) gastrointestinal tract dissections using already-deceased specimens and (2) non-invasive fecal sampling via bird banding. We found that bird banding with diet analysis was a comparable non‑lethal approach to achieve larger datasets for immediate micrplastic ingestion. The GI tract results suggest long‑term retention in these species. We found that MP loads are more influenced by fine-scale local variation on individuals rather than ecological characteristics like diet or foraging strategy, or morphological variation like body size. This research is currently in review—stay tuned! For more information on the influence and trajectories of microplastics in avian communities, please read more in Elias and Corbin (2025) Ornithology.
